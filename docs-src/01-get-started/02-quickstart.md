This walks you from an empty account to your first set of answers. Onboarding asks for the same things in the same order, so you can follow along in the portal.

## 1. Create a project

A project is one brand on one website. Give it a name and your main domain, for example `fernhill.example`.

## 2. Check your brand profile

We read your site and draft a short [brand profile](/docs/brand-profile/): what you sell, who buys it, the markets you serve, and a list of facts about you. Nothing is saved until you confirm it, so fix anything that's wrong. The facts list matters later, because [fact-checking](/docs/fact-checking/) compares what assistants say against it.

## 3. Pick topics

Topics are the themes your prompts are grouped under, such as "Subscriptions" or "Brewing gear". We suggest a few from your profile. Keep the ones that match how you think about your market, rename or add your own. See [Topics, tags and personas](/docs/topics-tags-personas/).

## 4. Add prompts

Generate prompts, write your own, or import a CSV. Aim for **at least 10 active prompts** to start. When generating you can choose:

- **Branded share**: how many prompts mention your brand by name. Default 20%.
- **Intent mix**: default 25% informational, 50% commercial, 25% transactional.

Generated prompts arrive as *suggested*. Only *active* prompts are run, so activate the ones you want. [Writing good prompts](/docs/writing-prompts/) has tips.

## 5. Confirm competitors

Add the brands you compete with, plus any aliases people use for them. We'll also suggest brands we spot in answers later. See [Competitors and aliases](/docs/competitors-and-aliases/).

## 6. Choose engines and countries

Turn on the engines you care about and the countries you sell in. Every active prompt runs on every enabled engine for every selected country, so this choice drives [usage](/docs/usage/).

## 7. Finish

Finishing onboarding starts your first pass straight away. A live progress bar shows answers as they complete. After that, passes run daily at 06:00 UTC unless you change the [schedule](/docs/project-settings/).

> **Before you share results with anyone**, check the engine list for any engine marked **stub**. A stub engine means no API key is configured for it, and it produces sample answers for demos and testing. Stub answers count in your metrics like any other, so add real keys first. See [Engines reference](/docs/engines-reference/).

## What to look at first

Once the pass is done, open the [Overview](/docs/overview/), then read a handful of [chats](/docs/reading-chats/) end to end. Reading real answers is the fastest way to see whether your prompts are asking the right questions.
