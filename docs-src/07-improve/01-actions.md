Actions turn what we've measured into a short list of things to do. Each one comes with the evidence behind it and a rough impact score.

## Kinds of action

| Action | What it's about |
|---|---|
| Get listed | A domain cites your competitors but not you. Getting onto that site could change future answers. |
| Refresh page | One of your existing pages could do a better job of being cited, for example because it's out of date. |
| New page | Assistants answer a question you have no page for, and cite someone else. |
| Fix crawl | AI crawlers aren't fetching a page that matters, such as a cited page they stopped visiting. |
| Fix fact | Assistants repeat a claim about you that contradicts your [facts](/docs/fact-checking/). |

## Status

Move each action through four states:

| Status | Use it when |
|---|---|
| Open | Not started |
| In progress | Someone's working on it |
| Done | Finished. We record when |
| Dismissed | Not worth doing, or not true |

Marking things done builds a record of what you changed. When a metric moves a few weeks later, you can look back and see what you did before it.

## Evidence

Every action links to what caused it: the chats, domains, pages or crawler data. Read it before you act. Like insights, actions point you somewhere worth looking. They aren't instructions.

## Impact

Impact is a rough score for how much an action might matter. Use it to sort, not as a forecast.

## Content briefs

For new page and refresh page actions, you can ask for a [content brief](/docs/content-briefs/).
